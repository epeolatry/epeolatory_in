### **Blurb:**
“Long Live Revolution!

Down with Imperialism!”

Bhagat Singh, a figure both feared and revered by the imperial regime, was not just a revolutionary but a luminary whose words stirred souls and kindled the flames of patriotism during India’s Independence movement. Despite his tender age of 23, he wielded his pen with the potency of a seasoned philosopher, leaving an indelible mark on the minds of millions.

In this riveting collection, explore the fiery prose and fearless spirit of Bhagat Singh through his impassioned letters and essays, including Why I am an Atheist, Religion and Our Freedom Struggle, To Young Political Workers, and others that shook the foundations of colonial oppression.

Bhagat Singh’s voice resonates from the confines of his prison cell where he faced the gallows with unyielding courage. His words continue to echo, demystifying his revolutionary fervour and illuminating the path to liberation.

Through Why I Am an Atheist: Essays and Other Works, follow the journey of a martyr, a thinker, and a beacon of hope whose words continue to inspire and ignite masses even today.

### **My Review:**
“Inquilab Zindabad”

“Long Live Revolution!”

For Bhagat Singh, the idea of revolution meant complete freedom. He was against any government ruling over the public. Rather, he believed that humans were more than capable of living freely without the intervention of any authority. In his final years, he read, wrote, and spoke extensively about his idea of revolution and how the masses could help achieve it.

What I truly love about this book is the parallels visible between the 1930s and 2026. Almost a century later, the state of politics remains much the same. Whether it was the Britishers, the blooming idea of Congress as a political force, or the current government, Bhagat Singh accurately recognised how those in power used divide-and-rule tactics, controlled the media, and silenced dissent to maintain their authority. At just 23, he not only stood by his ideology but also spread his ideas among the masses, encouraging others to take to the streets in protest and revolt.

While I may not be entirely convinced by the idea of absolute freedom, I agree that governance has not changed much since the British ruled us.

The essays are thought-provoking, and at a time like this, marked by global unrest and frequent protests, the book is certainly an eye-opener.

As readers, we may have differing opinions on what freedom should mean to us, but if there is one thing we can take away from this book, it is that we should never stop questioning those in power. Freedom of any kind has rarely been given; it has almost always had to be snatched.

Suitable For Age: 15+
